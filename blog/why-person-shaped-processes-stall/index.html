Why processes built around people stall when you add AI

Most companies add AI by giving each person an assistant. The process stays the same, and so does its bottleneck.

Sergey Sheleg, co-founder of PassionCode.ai

The person is still the bottleneck

An assistant drafts and analyzes, then hands the result back to a person. Ten assistants hand back ten results. The work waits until that person has time to read them.

Meetings reconcile what the assistants made

Two people with their own assistants produce more than either can read. They meet to agree on it, and the process stays exactly as it was.

Everyone builds their own agents

No shared rules, no shared memory, keys pasted wherever someone works, and no record of what an agent did or what it cost. It adds work instead of removing it.

Most companies now experiment with agents, and few run them across a whole function (McKinsey, The State of AI, November 2025). Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be canceled by the end of 2027 (Gartner, June 2025).

What changes

The process itself has to move. When work moves between agents instead of between people, an agent takes the task and hands it to the next agent, and they settle it on one board with the record. People keep what only people can do: direction, control and expertise. More work then means more agents, not more people.
